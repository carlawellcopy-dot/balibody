Who they are

The brand, in one read

A category leader in affordable, skincare led self tan, born from a sensitive skin problem and scaled through social. Acquisition and trust are clearly solved. The opening for us sits in retention: turning a huge, proven, one time buyer base into a repeat and loyalty machine across two regions we run, the US and the EU.

Founded in Melbourne. Mature brand, still no subscription in the mix.

Founders & origin

Co-founded by Laura Foley and David Oosterloo in Melbourne. Laura lived with eczema and sensitive skin, and the pair saw few affordable suncare and tanning options for reactive skin. The first product was a tanning and body oil; the range grew from there. The sensitive skin origin is the emotional root of the "skin first" positioning.

Positioning & moat

"A new philosophy on skin first self tanning" and "skincare infused self tan." They sit above mass drugstore tan (Bondi Sands, b.tan) on formulation and feel, and below prestige (St. Tropez) on price. Moat is brand love, a giant review base, Australian made credibility, and a very wide format range.

Audience

Primarily women who want a natural, streak free, sun kissed glow without UV damage, plus a suncare and skincare crossover buyer. Values cues on site: vegan, cruelty free, naturally derived, suitable for all skin types, Australian made. Aesthetic driven, results led, review reassured.

Business model

One time purchase DTC on Shopify, five regional stores (US, AU/NZ, CA, UK/International, EU), plus retail and marketplace presence (Ulta, Amazon). Consumable products with a natural reorder rhythm, but no subscribe and save is offered today. That gap is the headline retention opportunity.

Programs already in place

BB Air rewards (Yotpo powered, aviation tiers: Economy, Business, First Class, Private Jet), a Tan Finder quiz, a Notify Me back in stock capture, an Okendo reviews hub, and content in Guides & Tips and the BB Blog. Klaviyo access confirms these already feed email: the Yotpo Loyalty Welcome and the Tan Finder flow are among the top earning flows, and Back In Stock is live. The lifecycle engine exists and is running.

The tension

A premium feeling, skincare led brand that leans on markdowns to convert (an always on EU Sale collection, around 22 products at capture and deeper at seasonal peaks, oils cut roughly 30 percent, value sets at a standing discount). The brand story and the discount behaviour pull against each other, and campaigns earn far less than flows, so the promo habit is not even paying off in broadcast. Room to retain with ritual and value, not just price.

Scope note. Bali Body is Australian, but we oversee the US and EU stores only. Everything below is read through those two storefronts. The EU store is the more complex of the two: eleven display languages and a shared euro price across many countries. That makes localisation and region split sends a first order concern, not an afterthought.

Why it works / where it leaks

Strong
  • Multiple shopping logics. Shade, format, and concern paths mean any buyer intent has a route in.
  • Quiz as a front door. The Tan Finder is a natural zero party data capture for shade, skin type, and goal.
  • Value ladder is visible. Singles, value sets, and premium gift bundles are all merchandised clearly.
  • Trust everywhere. Review counts in the thousands sit on hero cards; badges reinforce vegan and cruelty free.
  • BNPL lowers the barrier. Afterpay and Sezzle on US, Klarna on EU, matched to each region.
Where it leaks
  • No subscription. A consumable range with no subscribe and save leaves repeat revenue on the table.
  • Notify Me is live but under monetised. Back In Stock is an automated, revenue tracked flow, but it earns only about 2.8k US and 1.7k EU a year against frequent sold out heroes. Upside, not a gap.
  • Quiz feeds email, depth unclear. The Tan Finder flow is live and a top earner (9.4k US, 27.5k EU), so the quiz does write to Klaviyo. Whether shade and skin type drive ongoing personalisation is the open question.
  • EU localisation risk. Eleven site languages, but are the emails localised, or is the EU list sent one generic language?
  • Discount habit. Standing markdowns train buyers to wait for a sale, which erodes full price demand.
Regions we run. US: free shipping over 45 dollars, Afterpay and Sezzle. EU: free shipping over 35 euro, Klarna, and a language switcher covering English, Dutch, French, German, Italian, Spanish, Swedish, Norwegian, Finnish, Danish, and Hebrew. Send timing, offer thresholds, currency, and language all differ by region, so US and EU should be planned as two coordinated calendars, not one.

Where the money is

Opportunities we would own

Reprioritised against the live data. The flows already exist, so the money is in the flows that under earn, in campaigns, in SMS, and in closing the US gap to EU. Ranked by likely revenue impact, each tied to a real number from their account.

1 · Fix campaigns biggest lever

Campaigns earn only ~32% of Klaviyo revenue with thin click through, despite carrying the discount load. Better segmentation, subject lines, and a considered promo calendar can lift the weakest part of the program in both regions.

2 · Replenishment & subscribe to save big lever

The replenishment flow is live but earns just $2k US and $0.8k EU. On a consumable range with no subscription, rebuilding replenishment on real fade and run out timing, plus a subscribe and save option, is the clearest LTV lift.

3 · Close the US gap to EU quantified

US Klaviyo share is 14.3% vs EU 21.1% on a bigger store. Bringing US to EU levels alone is roughly a $300k a year swing. Start with the flows and campaigns that already outperform in EU.

4 · Scale the mid tier flows quick win

Back in stock ($2.8k / $1.7k) and EU post purchase ($2.2k) are live but tiny against the traffic behind them. Sequencing, timing, and cross sell can multiply these without building anything new.

5 · Activate SMS channel

SMS flow clones exist (welcome, cart, checkout, browse, winback) but earn almost nothing today. A properly built SMS channel, capped alongside email, is an untapped revenue line.

6 · EU localisation region

Eleven site languages on the EU store. Confirm whether sends are localised or one generic version, then segment the EU list by market and language and localise at least the top clusters.

7 · Deepen quiz & loyalty personalisation asset ready

Tan Finder and BB Air already earn (quiz $9k US / $27k EU; loyalty welcome ~$50k each). Push shade, skin type, and tier further into welcome, cross sell, and campaigns to compound what already works.

8 · Flow hygiene & hemisphere calendar housekeeping

Clean the 84 to 88 flow draft sprawl (duplicate BFCM and Boxing Day builds, TEST flows), then run US and EU as two coordinated calendars that exploit opposite summers for the new SPF50 and oils.

The ones that make the room pause

Sharper questions

The Questions tab gathers the facts we need. These are different: fewer, sharper, and built to show senior thinking and make them see their own business from a new angle. Pick two or three that fit the room. They are designed to open a conversation, not to be run as a checklist.

Economics they may not be looking at

If you switched off the Sale collection for thirty days, how much revenue actually disappears versus simply shifts to full price?

Forces an honest read on discount dependence. Most brands assume the sale drives demand; often it just subsidises buyers who would have paid anyway.

Is a customer who orders a second time worth two times a one time buyer to you, or ten times? Do you know?

Tests whether they measure LTV at all. If the second order is where the margin lives, retention stops being a nice to have and becomes the growth plan.

You built a consumable brand with no subscription. Was that a deliberate brand decision, an operational blocker, or just never the priority?

The answer tells us exactly how to pitch replenishment and subscribe to save, and whether the barrier is belief, tech, or focus.

You have grown huge since 2014 without a heavy lifecycle program. Why invest in retention now?

Surfaces the real pressure behind the brief, whether rising acquisition costs, a plateau, or a margin squeeze. That is the goal we should actually be solving for.

Tensions worth naming out loud

Your brand says skin first, premium, ritual. Your calendar says sale. Which is the real Bali Body, and which do you want us to amplify?

Names the core tension in the whole account. Their answer sets the strategic north star for every send we build.

When a hero sells out, do you see lost revenue or captured demand? What happens to that Notify Me list right now?

Reframes stockouts from a problem into an owned, high intent audience. Reveals whether scarcity is being monetised or wasted.

Is your EU customer a different person than your US customer, or the same person in a different currency?

Tests how deeply they understand region. It opens the localisation conversation and how differently we should speak to, say, Germany versus France versus the Nordics.

You sell summer in two hemispheres. Is that a smoothing advantage you actively use, or does the business still breathe with one calendar?

Most brands never exploit opposite seasons across US and EU. If they are not, that is a revenue evening lever hiding in plain sight.

Assets they are sitting on

The Tan Finder already knows a customer's shade, skin type, and goal before they buy. If I asked you to send an email using all three today, could you?

Exposes the gap between data collected and data used. If the quiz does not write to the profile, personalisation is blind and that is a fast, visible win.

You have thousands of reviews on a single product. Where does that customer voice show up outside the product page?

A giant proof asset that rarely makes it into email, winback, or objection handling. Underused social proof is free conversion.

What is the most valuable product to product journey you have, and are you actively engineering it, or just hoping for it?

Moves cross sell from guesswork to a designed path. They already have a manual cross sell map in the backend; this asks whether anyone is driving it.

If BB Air disappeared tomorrow, would your best customers actually notice, or is it just a discount on repeat buyers?

A provocative loyalty audit. Separates a program that changes behaviour from one that quietly erodes margin.

Aligning on the win

Twelve months from now, what has to be true for you to say hiring us was obviously the right call?

Pins the definition of success to something concrete: a revenue number, a flow that did not exist, a metric they cannot move today. Everything we build points back here.

What do you wish you knew about your customers that you do not collect today?

Turns them into a partner in the plan and reveals the zero party data gaps we should start closing from day one.

How to use these. Two or three, placed well, do more than a full list. Lead with a tension question to set the strategic frame, follow with an economics question to ground it in money, and close with the twelve month question to align on the outcome we will be judged on.

Tentpole ramp

Seasonal (both hemispheres), Black Friday and Cyber, holiday gift sets, and product launches run as tease, launch, and last chance beats rather than one off blasts.

How we would slice the base

Segmentation starting point

We now have account access, and the base is already heavily segmented: 52 segments and 14 lists in US, 54 segments and 12 lists in EU. So this is less "build segmentation" and more "rationalise the sprawl and make sure every send is filtered by lifecycle, engagement, and value, with region and shade or format affinity layered on." The model below is the target state to organise toward.

Stage 1
Lead / pre purchase

Quiz takers and subscribers who have not bought. Welcome and first order.

Stage 2
First time buyer

Orders one and two, highest churn. Onboarding and reorder.

Stage 3
Established

Repeat buyers. Cross sell into face tan, suncare, BB Skin.

Stage 4
VIP / high value

Top spenders and BB Air members. Status and early access.

Stage 5
At risk

Slipping past the reorder window. Replenishment and save.

Stage 6
Lapsed

No order in the danger zone. Winback, then sunset.
